A teaching app that lets students place buried bodies under real topography and watch the gravity
or magnetic anomaly change as they work. A companion app, Gravis2D, adds fast 2-D inversion.

![Gravity stations over Doi Inthanon (Copernicus GLO-30 DEM), coloured in mGal above a buried body](/images/gravis.jpg)
*Gravity stations over Doi Inthanon (Copernicus GLO-30 DEM), coloured in mGal above a buried body.*

### For the classroom

- **Seven body types** — sphere, prism, two cylinders, dipping sheet, faulted slab and a drawn polygon, placed with the mouse, with undo and redo.
- **Real terrain** — a bundled DEM of Doi Inthanon, your own GeoTIFF, or a download from OpenTopography. A terrain-correction view splits the total field into terrain effect and bodies.
- **Instant feedback** — the sensitivity matrix is built once, so every edit updates the anomaly immediately.
- **Gravity or magnetics** — the same bodies in mGal or as total-field magnetic anomaly in nT.
- **Non-uniqueness, shown** — smooth and sparse inversions side by side, and a compare mode with two different models that produce the same data.
- **Exports** — report figures, CSV data with seeded noise for class exercises, scenario files, and VTK for ParaView.

Gravis2D adds 2-D gravity and magnetic inversion with ready-made scenarios.

Python, SimPEG and pyGIMLi, running as a local web app; MIT licence; about 740 automated tests across the two apps.
