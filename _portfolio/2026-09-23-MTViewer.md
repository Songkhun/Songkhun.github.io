Two tools for working with 3-D magnetotelluric (MT) inversion results: a desktop viewer for
checking and interpreting a model, and a Blender add-on for publication figures and animations.

![A 3-D resistivity model rendered with the Blender add-on: three orthogonal sections and the MT stations](/images/mtviewer.jpg)
*A 3-D resistivity model rendered with the Blender add-on: three orthogonal sections and the MT stations.*

### Desktop viewer

- Reads WSINV3DMT and ModEM models and data, and detects the format automatically.
- Map, section, pseudo-section, sounding curve, RMS and 3-D views.
- Cross-sections along any line you draw, also shown as a curtain in 3-D.
- Overlays faults, geology and stations from MAT, CSV, shapefile or KML (including Google Earth exports).
- Exports PNG, SVG and PDF at slide sizes, plus orbit and depth-sweep videos (MP4 or GIF) for presentations.

### Blender add-on

- Exact cell-based sections and threshold surfaces around conductive bodies.
- Figure and turntable renders, also from the command line.
- Writes a provenance file with source hashes and settings for every render, so a figure can be traced back to its model.

Python (PySide6, PyVista, matplotlib) and Blender Python; about 170 automated tests.
